Extract board game reviews from the article below.

You are given a <source> header describing the page, then sometimes one or more
<review> blocks, then the article itself in <article>.

<source> describes the page you are reading — its url, title, site, and whatever
byline, medium, tags or categories a scraper read from it. It is not a review.
In a roundup that cites other critics, the page's own site, url and date belong
to none of them — never copy them into outlet_name, review_url, published_in or
published_at.

A <review> block is different. It is what a scraper already worked out about one
review on this page: its critic, the outlet it appeared in, its own url and
date, the game it is about. These are established facts — use them. When
<review> blocks are present, extract one review per block and fill each field
from the block where the block gives it, from the article for the rest (the
quote, the sentiment, the rating, and anything the block leaves out). The
article may hold more reviews than there are blocks — extract those too. When
there are no <review> blocks, the article is all you have.

The article keeps its HTML. Use that structure: <p> bounds one passage,
<strong> and headings mark names, <a href> is a real link, and <blockquote>
marks a quotation.

For each distinct (game, reviewer) pair found, extract:

- game: the game under review, as an object:
    - title: the title as the article or a <review> block names it, including
      any subtitle — prefer the form used in the body over the headline's
      ("Endeavor: Die Tiefsee" rather than "Endeavor")
    - designers: every designer the article or a <review> block names, as
      written; [] if none
    - publishers: every publisher named; a co-edition is two entries, so
      "Frosted Games / Board Game Circus" is ["Frosted Games", "Board Game
      Circus"]; [] if none
    - year_published: the game's year of publication as four digits, only if the
      article or a <review> block states it; null otherwise
    - bgg_url: the game's boardgamegeek.com address, only if it is linked or
      cited; null otherwise
- reviewer_name: the reviewer's name as written, or as a <review> block's
  `critic` line gives it; use the outlet name if no individual is named
- outlet_name: the publication or outlet this reviewer published in, when the
  article or a <review> block names it for this reviewer; null otherwise. Where
  an article lists several critics and several outlets, do not pair them up by
  order or proximity — attribute an outlet only where it is tied to that
  reviewer
- medium: how the review itself was published — one of: text / video / podcast /
  print / social; null if it is not clear. A <review> block's `medium` line, or
  a link to a video or podcast host, is good evidence
- published_in: where the review appeared, as the article or a <review> block
  describes it, including issue or episode details (e.g. "Spielbox 3/2024, S.
  42", "Brettagogen #276"); null if not stated. This is a place, never a date
- review_url: the address of the reviewer's own review, if it is linked or cited
  (a <review> block's `review url` line, or a link in the article); null
  otherwise. It must point at the review itself — not the outlet's front page,
  not a podcast's channel page, and not the page you are reading
- published_at: the date the reviewer's own review appeared, as YYYY-MM-DD, if
  the article or a <review> block states it; null otherwise. This is the
  review's date, not the date of the page you are reading. Convert local
  formats: German "25.4.26" is 2026-04-25 and "4. Mai 2025" is 2025-05-04. If
  only a month, a year or an issue number is given, leave this null and record
  that detail in published_in
- raw_score: a score stated in the article's own prose (e.g. "4/5", "8 out of
  10", "sehr gut"), copied as written; null if the prose states no score. The
  scores in <review> blocks are already recorded separately — do not repeat them
  here, and a <review> block's `rating` lines are not prose
- rating: a 1–10 integer for the reviewer's verdict. The scale is deliberately
  lopsided — most of it describes games worth playing, because that is where the
  useful distinctions lie. Judge by what the text does, not by how enthusiastic it
  sounds:
    10:  a landmark; ranked among the best the reviewer knows
     9:  exceptional; recommended without reservation
     8:  great; recommended warmly, any reservations are minor
     7:  strong; recommended, with reservations worth stating
     6:  good; recommended for the right player
     5:  okay; would play again but does not press it on anyone
     4:  would not play again, though it has real merits
     3:  poor; recommends against it
   1–2:  nothing to salvage
- sentiment: one of: negative / mixed_negative / neutral / mixed_positive / positive
- quote: one continuous passage of the reviewer's own words, copied exactly, at
  most 300 characters; null if there is none
- language: ISO 639-1 two-letter code for the language of the text you are quoting,
  not the language the critic originally published in (e.g. "de", "en")

Rules:
- Extract every (game, reviewer) pair present, even if a single article covers multiple
  games or multiple reviewers.
- If a reviewer is not named but an opinion is clearly attributed, use the outlet name
  as reviewer_name.
- A quote must be one continuous run of text, copied character for character. Do
  not join two fragments, and do not carry the article author's narration between
  them: `„…", schreibt Bartsch. „…"` is two quotes and a splice, not a quote.
- Quote only the reviewer's own words. Where the article's author reports what a
  critic said rather than quoting them — in German the Konjunktiv, "Das Spiel
  biete ihm nichts Neues" — that is a paraphrase, not a quote. Leave quote null
  rather than passing a paraphrase off as verbatim.
- Do not include the quotation marks that surround a quote.
- If the best passage runs past 300 characters, choose a shorter passage that
  fits. Never truncate one: a cut-off sentence is no longer what the reviewer
  wrote.
- If no passage of the reviewer's own words exists, set quote to null. That is a
  normal outcome, not a failure.
- The line runs between 4 and 5: 5 is the lowest rating for a game the reviewer would
  play again, 4 the highest for one they would not. Settle that question first, then
  choose within the half it falls in.
- A conflicted review is not automatically a 5. Decide whether the recommendation
  survives the criticism; if it does, rate 5 or above, and if it does not, rate 4 or
  below.
- Rate the verdict, not the reviewer's taste. "A brilliant design that I personally
  find exhausting" is a strong verdict, not a lukewarm one.
- Treat comparisons as relative. "Better than its predecessor" says nothing on its own
  about where the game belongs on the scale.
- Give a rating whether or not a score is stated. Where a score exists — in the
  prose or a <review> block — judge the verdict independently rather than
  restating the number; the two are checked against each other later.
- Text that describes a game without judging it — a preview, an announcement, a rules
  summary, a list of what is in the box — is not a review. Do not extract it.
- Never invent or guess year_published, bgg_url, outlet_name, published_in,
  review_url or published_at. You may recognise the game and know its year or its
  BoardGameGeek page from elsewhere; that is not what is being asked. Report only
  what the article or its <review> blocks state, and null otherwise. medium is
  the exception: reading it off a link's destination is not guessing.
- A review with no link is normal — print reviews have no address at all. Record what
  the article does say in published_in rather than forcing a url.
- If the article contains no board game reviews, return an empty list.
